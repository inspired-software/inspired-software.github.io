Where we stand

Pay once, and it does not vanish when we do

Two things we intend about how our software is sold, and what happens to it if we ever stop. They are here because they are easier to say now than to be argued out of later.

A licence you keep

We would rather sell software than rent it. That is the intention for everything we make: a licence you buy once and keep, with no monthly fee and no feature you already paid for moving behind a tier later.

Lifetime, if you want the word for it, means the app’s life — not yours, and not ours: you go on using what you bought for as long as it runs, and the fixes and improvements we make to it are included. If some later version is ever a big enough piece of new work to stand on its own, we will say so plainly, and the copy you already have will carry on regardless. What we will not do is quietly restart the meter on something you have already paid for.

If we stop, the code goes out

If we ever stop a product for good — off sale with nothing of ours replacing it, or the company itself winding up — we publish the code we wrote for it under an open licence: a real one, that lets anyone use it, change it and ship it. Software you paid for should not become unusable because the people who made it moved on.

That happens as part of stopping rather than as a favour afterwards, and if it is the company that ends, the code goes out before the lights do. It covers the in-house libraries underneath the app as well, because code nobody can build is not much of a gift. And it survives a sale: if the product or the company ever changes hands, this goes with it. A release running late is not stopping, and neither is replacing an old version with one we still look after. Today none of it is published: the libraries under the app are ours and internal, and this is about what happens if we stop, not something to go and download now.

One honest limit: other people’s work stays under the licence it came with, and anything we ever take under someone else’s agreement is theirs to hand on rather than ours. The name and the artwork stay ours too. Everything we wrote, you get.

The fine print, which we would rather say than have you assume. This is a philosophy, not a legally binding promise. The formal terms of any purchase are whatever licence you agree to at the time, and nothing on this page adds to that licence or stands in for reading it. What is written here is the intent behind it, set down in public and in plain words, so it is easy to check and hard for us to quietly drop.
