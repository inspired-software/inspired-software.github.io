Legal

Privacy

We run no servers. There is no copy of your dive log anywhere but your own device and your own iCloud account — beyond a dive you deliberately share — which means most of the questions a privacy policy usually dances around have a short answer here.

Last updated 7 August 2026

The short version

  • There is no Inspired Software account. Nothing to create, nothing to log into, no password of ours to lose.
  • Deeplife keeps your data on your device and mirrors it to your iCloud account. We cannot read it.
  • No analytics, no tracking, no advertising, no profiling. We sell nothing and share nothing, because we hold nothing.
  • Photos and video stay on your device and in your iCloud. Species suggestions are not trained yet, so nothing is suggested today — and when they are, the work will happen on your own device. Either way, no image is sent anywhere to be looked at.
  • Proximity search works from a place name you type, geocoded by Apple. Nothing about where you are is collected in the background.
  • If you email us, we keep the email so we can answer it.

The rest of this page is the long version. It covers Deeplife on iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Vision Pro and Apple TV — the first release — and it covers this website. It also covers the Apple Watch app, which is planned to follow the first release and is not part of it; where something applies only to the watch, this page says so at that point rather than leaving you to work it out. Deeplife is unreleased and in active development; if something we build changes what this page has to say, the page changes with it.

Where your data lives

The copy on your device is the real one — that is how the app is built, before it is a privacy promise. Everything you put in — dives, photos, videos, edits, tags, presets, ratings, favourites — is written to a single file on your device, with the photo and video files themselves kept alongside it. The app works with no signal at all.

Sync mirrors all of that to the iCloud account you are already signed into. It goes into the private part of your own account. That is a part of iCloud we have no access to: Apple does not hand an app’s developer the contents of anyone’s private iCloud storage, and we have built nothing that would want it. Your iCloud storage is governed by your agreement with Apple and by Apple’s privacy policy, not by us.

On Apple TV the copy on the device sits in space the system is free to reclaim rather than in permanent storage, because it can always be rebuilt from your iCloud. The Apple TV app is for viewing; it does not accept share invitations.

What we collect

Nothing. That is not a turn of phrase, it is the shape of the product: there is no server of ours for anything to arrive at.

  • No account, no sign-up, no email address required to use the app.
  • No analytics tool, no crash-reporting service, no advertising identifier, no attribution or measurement kit, no third-party code that phones home.
  • No profiles, no inferences, no “interests” derived from what you photograph or where you dive.
  • Nothing sold, nothing rented, nothing shared with partners. There is no data set here to sell.

One honest footnote, for once Deeplife is released: if you have Apple’s own “Share With App Developers” setting turned on, Apple may show us aggregate, anonymised reports about crashes and usage. That is Apple’s system, you control it in your device’s privacy settings, and nothing in it identifies a person. We add no measurement of our own on top.

Photos and video

Imported photos and videos are stored on your device with the rest of the log and mirror to your iCloud with everything else. Small thumbnails travel with each photo, so grids fill in instantly on every device; full-size files download from your own iCloud when you actually open them.

Edits are non-destructive. A crop, a colour grade, a heal spot, a mask — each is stored as settings attached to the photo and applied when the photo is shown. The original file is never modified and never replaced.

Camera metadata (camera and lens, focal length, aperture, shutter, ISO, flash, original dimensions, and GPS coordinates if the camera recorded them) is read from the original file at import and kept with the photo, because the copy Deeplife stores is re-saved at a working size and would otherwise lose it. You can see it in the photo’s Info sheet. It syncs the way the rest of your log syncs: to your iCloud, not to us.

Identifying species

Deeplife’s species suggestions are not trained yet, so the app suggests nothing today. Everything around the feature is built and in place, but until it has been trained it says so plainly rather than guessing at species it was never taught. What is settled is where it will run once it does work: entirely on your own device. No image will be uploaded to be looked at, by us or by anyone else, and no connection will be needed to suggest a species.

The training set is 774 commercial-use images across the 120 catalogued species, gathered and vetted separately and never shipped inside the app. It is not built from anybody’s library. Your photos are not used to train anything of ours, and there is no mechanism by which they could be — they never reach us. If that ever changes it will be something you opt into, and this page will say so before it does.

Location

Finding dive sites near you is something you ask for, one search at a time. You give the app a place — a city, an island, a dive town — or pick a destination from the built-in directory, and it works out what lies within 25 to 500 km. Place names are geocoded through Apple Maps, so that lookup goes to Apple under Apple’s policy. Nothing about where you are is collected in the background, and nothing is stored for the purpose of that search after it is done.

Dives themselves carry coordinates, because a dive happened somewhere. Those are stored on the dive itself — yours, stored and synced exactly like the depth and the date — and they are what makes “dives near here” work. Dive-site pages can show an Apple Maps view of the site, which loads map data from Apple; the seafloor chart beside it is drawn from bathymetry that ships inside the app, so it makes no request at all.

Exporting

When you export a photo, metadata — GPS included — is stripped by default. Keeping it is a deliberate choice you make in the export options, per export. We picked that default because the coordinates of a shore entry, or of the house you took the picture from, are not something to leak by accident.

Once the file is handed to the system share sheet it is out of the app’s hands and into whatever you sent it to.

Sharing a dive

Share a dive and a copy of that dive and its photos is written into a separate shared area in your own iCloud account, and iCloud gives you a link. It is worth being blunt about what that means: anyone who has the link can view that dive and its photos. The link is not a password and it does not know who you meant to give it to. Recipients need Deeplife installed and tap the link to accept.

  • Sharing is read-only in the first release. Recipients can view; they cannot edit or delete, and Apple’s servers enforce that rather than the app asking nicely.
  • Only what you shared is shared. The rest of your log stays in the private part of your account and is not reachable from the link.
  • Edits you make afterwards are re-copied into the shared area, so what link-holders see follows what you have saved.

We are not a party to any of this. The shared copy lives in your iCloud account and moves between Apple’s servers and the recipient’s device; we neither hold it nor see who opened it.

Apple Health

Health sync belongs to the Apple Watch app, which is planned to follow the first release. Nothing Deeplife ships in the first release writes to Apple Health or asks for permission to. What follows is the commitment that will hold the day the watch arrives, written down now so it cannot be quietly loosened later.

Writing finished dives to Apple Health will be optional and off until you switch it on — a toggle on the watch, where a dive gets recorded. It will request write-only authorisation: Deeplife will write Underwater Diving workouts with their depth and water-temperature series, and will not read your health data. What it writes goes into Health on your device, under Apple’s rules for it, and you will be able to revoke the permission in Health at any time.

One part of that is not conditional on anything and never will be: nothing from HealthKit is transmitted to us. Not now, and not when the watch ships — there is no server of ours for it to arrive at.

Third-party content in the app

Deeplife bundles other people’s work in two places, and one of them makes a network request worth disclosing plainly.

Reference photos. The field guide carries one openly-licensed photograph per species — public domain, CC0, CC BY, or CC BY-SA — with author, licence, and source recorded for each. They ship inside the app. Browsing the field guide makes no request, so nobody learns which species you looked up.

Shipwreck models. Seventy Great Lakes wrecks have a photogrammetry model on their site page. Thirty of them are licensed CC BY and render natively on your device — fully offline, spatial on Apple Vision Pro, no network involved. The rest are under CC BY-ND, which permits a faithful format conversion but not redistributing a reduced one, so they are shown unmodified through the publisher’s own viewer, hosted on Sketchfab. That means opening one of those models makes a request to Sketchfab’s servers, exactly as loading a web page would, and Sketchfab sees that request under its own privacy policy. If you would rather not make it, do not open those models; every other part of the site page works without them.

Attribution for both, per item, appears in the app’s acknowledgements — the About window on Mac, and the Deeplife entry in Settings on iPhone, iPad, and Apple Vision Pro.

This website

These are plain pages, the same for everyone, served over HTTPS. There are no cookies, no analytics, no trackers, and nothing loaded from anyone else’s site — no outside scripts, no outside fonts, no outside images, and nothing that quietly counts visits on someone else’s behalf. Everything the page loads comes from this domain.

One thing is stored in your browser: your light-or-dark choice, kept there so the site does not flash the wrong theme on the next visit. It never leaves your browser and it is not an identifier.

The site is hosted on GitHub Pages, with Cloudflare in front of it directing the domain and delivering the pages. Both necessarily process the request that delivers the page to you — your IP address and which browser you are using among it — because that is what serving a file over the internet involves, and both do so under their own policies. We run no logging of our own. Cloudflare’s dashboard shows us aggregate traffic counts, as it does for every site it fronts; there is nothing per-person in it and we do nothing with it.

Links to other sites are just links. Follow one and you are on someone else’s site, under their policy.

Email

Email is how you reach us, and every “get in touch” button on this site is a plain email link that opens your own mail app. There is no form, no waitlist database, no newsletter platform, and no third party in between collecting addresses. We also keep a Mastodon account, linked in the footer of every page; anything you send us there travels under Mastodon’s policy rather than this one.

If you write to us, we keep your message so we can answer it. If you asked to be told when Deeplife ships, we keep your address until we send that one message. That is the whole use. You are not added to a list, your address is not passed to anyone, and there is no second email. Ask us to delete your correspondence and we will.

Your data, and getting rid of it

There is no account to close. Beyond that:

  • Delete a dive or a photo in the app and the deletion syncs like any other change — the copy in your iCloud goes with it.
  • Delete the app and the copy on that device goes with it.
  • What is in your iCloud account is yours to manage with Apple’s own iCloud controls. The flip side of us having no access is that we cannot delete it for you.

If you live somewhere with a data-protection law that grants you rights of access, correction, portability, or erasure, the practical position is that you already hold all of them, because you hold the data — on your device and in your own iCloud account, where we cannot reach it. Email us anyway if you want something confirmed in writing.

Children

Deeplife is not directed at children under 13 and we do not knowingly collect anything from them. That is an easier promise than usual to keep, given that we do not knowingly collect anything from anyone.

Changes to this policy

If this page changes, the date at the top changes with it. Deeplife is still being built, so it is likely to gain sections rather than lose them. What will not happen is a quiet broadening: if we ever want to collect something, this page will say so first, and it will be something you choose.
