What to put in the report

The three things that decide whether we can reproduce it at all:

  • Platform and OS version. “iPhone, iOS 27.2” or “Mac, macOS 27.1”. The table below says where each device keeps its version.
  • What you did. The steps, in order, right up to the moment it went wrong. “Opened the dive, tapped Edit, dragged the curve” beats “the editor is broken”.
  • What happened, and what you expected. Both halves. Half the time the gap between them is the whole bug.

Also useful, when it applies:

  • Whether it happens every time or happened once.
  • For a sync or sharing problem: which devices, whether they are signed into the same iCloud account, and whether you had a connection.
  • A screenshot or a short screen recording. Crop out anything you would rather we did not see.

What to expect back

We are a very small studio. Mail is read by the people who wrote the code, and we answer as soon as we can — realistically days rather than minutes, and longer if we are underwater or heads-down on a release. We would rather say that than publish a 24-hour promise we could not keep.

If we cannot reproduce something we will say so and ask for more, rather than quietly closing it. If a fix has to wait for the next release, we will tell you that too.

Deeplife has not shipped yet: there is no App Store listing, no TestFlight build, and no date. Questions about what it will do are fair game, and so is “tell me when it is out”.

We will never ask for your password. Not your Apple Account, not your iCloud, not anything else. Deeplife has no account of its own to lock you out of — there is nothing we could reset for you even if we wanted to. Please do not put credentials in an email to us.

Diving safety

What Deeplife is not

This is the part of the page we would ask you to read even if you never write to us.

The dive computer is experimental and has not been validated for real diving. Never dive without a commercially certified dive computer.

The watch app is not in the first release. It is built and tested, it is planned to follow, and it has no date — so for now there is no version of it you could take into the water even if you wanted to. What follows is written in the present tense because the app exists, not because you can have it.

It runs Bühlmann ZH-L16C with gradient factors, tracks a smoothed ascent rate, and raises ascent-rate, ceiling, and deco alerts. All of that is real code with real tests behind it. None of it has been validated against a certified device, in a chamber, or in the water by anyone qualified to sign it off. It is not a dive computer in the sense your training used that word, and shipping it later will not have made it one.

So: wear your certified computer, plan the dive with it, and execute the dive on it. That holds now, and it will still hold on the day the watch app arrives. If the two ever disagree, the certified one is right and ours is the one to ignore. Never let an app — ours or anyone else’s — be your only ascent-rate or decompression reference. That includes the surface interval and no-fly countdown: ours is a tissue simulation, not a guarantee about your body.

A number on a site page is not a briefing

The dive-site directory records each site’s charted shallowest and deepest points and the training it actually asks for, against the 40 m recreational limit. That is reference material, gathered so you know what to read about and who to ask — it is not a plan, and it is not a substitute for training you hold or a briefing from the operator who dives that site every week.

It is graded rather than yes/no for a reason: a wreck standing upright can put its masts at 6 m and its keel at 60 m. The tier badge tells you what the site asks of a diver. It cannot tell you about the current on the day, the viz, the entry, the boat, or whether you are the person who should be in the water.

Recreational · 705 Rec & Tec · 59 Technical · 14 Deepest 70 m

The logbook is a record, not a plan

Deeplife logs dives you have already done: site, date, depth, duration, water temperature, buddies, notes, photos. It is a record. It does not plan a dive for you, does not compute a gas plan, and is not a stand-in for the tables or planning software your training taught you to use. Reading last year’s profile is not the same as having a plan for tomorrow’s.

Seafloor charts come from a global grid

The seafloor view draws real bathymetry — an 8 km patch per site from GMRT and GEBCO. Global grids resolve walls, dropoffs, and channels well, but rarely small features. The in-app legend says so rather than implying precision that is not there, and so do we: it is not a navigational chart, and nothing in it should be used to judge what is actually beneath you.

Questions

Frequently asked

Answers to what we actually get asked. Where the honest answer is “not yet”, it says that.

When is Deeplife out?

It is in active development and has not been released. There is no App Store listing, no TestFlight build, and no date — and we would rather say nothing than name a month and miss it.

If you want one email when it ships, ask for one. That is the whole mechanism: no waitlist, no newsletter.

What will it cost?

Not announced. We have not set a figure, and we are not going to guess at one in public before the app is finished.

The shape of it we can tell you: a licence you buy once and keep, rather than a subscription. That is the next question.

Is it a subscription?

No. What we mean to sell is a licence you buy once and keep — yours for as long as the app runs, with the fixes and improvements we make to it included. No monthly fee to open your own dive log, and no feature you already paid for moving behind a tier later.

Running no servers is what lets us say that. There is no recurring cost on our side that would have to become a recurring cost on yours. No price has been announced, so this is about how the app will be sold rather than what it will cost — and it is a philosophy rather than a legally binding promise. The longer version, with the fine print, is on the about page.

Which devices will it run on?

The first release is five: iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Vision Pro, and Apple TV. iPhone, iPad, Mac, and Vision Pro get the full editor; Apple TV is viewing-focused. Apple Watch is planned, not part of the first release, and an Android version is planned as well. Neither has a date.

It needs the 27-series systems — iOS 27, macOS 27, tvOS 27, visionOS 27. Anything older is not supported. The watch app, whenever it arrives, will want watchOS 27 and an Apple Watch Ultra: live depth and water temperature during a dive come from that watch’s depth sensor. The full list is in the tech specs.

Is the Apple Watch app in the first release?

No. It is built and tested — a dive computer on the wrist running Bühlmann ZH-L16C with gradient factors, live depth on an Apple Watch Ultra, NDL and deco readouts, ascent-rate and ceiling alerts, one-tap logging of the finished dive into the synced log, and optional Apple Health workouts — but it is not shipping in the first release and there is no date for it.

It is planned to follow the first release, and we would rather say nothing than name a date and miss it. Shipping it will not have made it a validated dive computer, so the warning arrives with it — the longer version is in diving safety, above.

The dive computer is experimental and has not been validated for real diving. Never dive without a commercially certified dive computer.
Is there an Android version?

Planned, but not in the first release. Deeplife is being built for Apple platforms first and that is what will ship first; a version for Android phones and Wear OS is intended to follow.

There is no date for it, and we would rather not invent one. If Android is what you dive with, say so at {{contact}} — it genuinely helps us judge how loudly people are asking.

Do I need an account?

No. There is nothing to sign up for and nothing to sign in to. Deeplife keeps your log on your device and mirrors it through your iCloud account — the one already signed in on the device. We run no servers, so there is no account of ours for you to have.

Does it work offline?

Yes, fully. The copy on your device is the real one; it mirrors through your own iCloud when there is a connection and catches up when there is not. Logging, editing, tagging, the field guide, the site directory, and the seafloor charts all work with no signal at all — which is most of a dive trip.

Two caveats, both about the files that are large on purpose. Small preview images travel with the dive itself, so grids fill instantly everywhere; full-size photos and videos download when you open them, so one you have never opened on this device needs a connection the first time. And the shipwreck models that stream from their publisher’s own viewer need one too — the 30 stored in the app spin without any connection at all.

Where are my photos stored?

On your device. The whole log lives in one file there, with the picture and video files kept alongside it — on iPhone, iPad, Mac, and Vision Pro. That copy is the real one; it mirrors to your own iCloud, and no copy of it ever reaches us.

Your original files are never modified. Edits — crop, grade, masks, heal spots — are saved as settings next to the photo and applied when it is shown, so “Remove Grade” always gets you back the untouched original.

Can I share a dive with someone?

Yes, through iCloud. Deeplife makes a separate copy of that one dive and its photos and hands you a link to send. The recipient needs Deeplife installed and taps the link to accept; the dive then appears on their device through their own iCloud. Nothing else in your log travels with it.

In the first release, whoever you share with can look but not change anything — and that is enforced by iCloud itself, not by the app being polite. If you edit a shared dive later, the shared copy is updated, so what they see is your latest saved version.

Can I import dives from my dive computer?

Planned, not shipping. The in-house decompression work behind the watch app — itself planned rather than part of the first release — already includes the part that talks to dive computers over Bluetooth, plus simulated computers to test it against. That is the groundwork for reading dives off your own computer on the other devices.

The groundwork existing is not the feature existing, and we are not going to imply otherwise. There is no date. When import does land, imported dives are meant to reach Apple Health exactly the same way watch-recorded ones will.

What does the app do on Apple TV?

Viewing, and only viewing: shelf-style browsing by recent, favourites, and site; dive galleries; and a full-screen slideshow that advances itself. No editing, no importing, no accepting shares.

On Apple TV the app’s copy of your log sits in space the system is free to clear out whenever it wants it back. That is deliberate: the Apple TV copy is treated as something that can always be rebuilt from your own iCloud, and the app downloads again whatever it needs. Nothing on Apple TV is ever the only copy of anything.

Is my data used to train anything?

No. Species suggestions are worked out on your own device by design — and today they do not happen at all, because nothing has been trained to make them yet. Either way, no photo is uploaded to us to be looked at: we run no servers to upload it to, and no copy of your library ever reaches us.

The species suggester ships unfinished and says so: until it has been trained it reports itself unavailable rather than producing confident noise. The photographs gathered to train it are a separate collection of openly licensed, commercial-use images — 774 of them across the 120 catalogued species, each with its source and licence recorded — kept out of the app entirely.

Can I get my photos back out?

Yes. Edited stills export as JPEG or HEIC with quality, size, and keep-or-strip-metadata options — GPS is stripped by default, so a dive site you would rather keep quiet stays that way. Videos export as MP4 with the crop and the grade baked into every frame. Single items or whole batches, handed to the system share sheet.

What happens if you stop working on it?

Start with the part that does not depend on us at all. Your log sits on your own device and in your own iCloud account — we hold no copy of it. If this studio went quiet tomorrow, nothing of yours would move: the dives stay where they already are, and the photos still export out on your own terms. Your logbook is not held hostage to us continuing to exist.

Then the part that is up to us. If we ever stop a product for good — off sale with nothing of ours replacing it, or the company itself winding up — we intend to publish the code we wrote for it under an open licence, along with the in-house libraries underneath it, so anyone who depends on it can keep it alive. None of that is public today: the condition is us stopping, and there is nothing to go looking for in the meantime. Other people’s work stays under the licence it came with: the reference photographs and the wreck scans travel on their own terms rather than ours. Everything we wrote, you get. That is a philosophy rather than a legally binding promise, and the whole of it, limits included, is on the about page.

Does it write dives to Apple Health?

That is part of the watch app, which is planned rather than in the first release — so not yet. When it arrives it will be optional and strictly opt-in: a toggle on the watch will request write-only authorisation, and a finished dive you save to the log will be written to Health as an Underwater Diving workout, with underwater-depth and water-temperature series. Leave the toggle off and nothing is written.

Does the watch app replace my dive computer?

No — and at the moment it could not, because it is not in the first release and there is no watch build to dive with. When it does arrive the answer is still no: it is experimental and has not been validated for real diving. Never dive without a commercially certified dive computer — the longer version is in diving safety, above.

Disclosure

Reporting a security or privacy issue

Same address — {{contact}} — with security in the subject line, so it sorts to the top of the pile. Please send it privately first rather than posting it, and give us a reasonable window to fix it before you publish.

What helps us act on it quickly:

  • What you found, and the steps to reproduce it.
  • What someone could actually do with it.
  • The platform and OS version you saw it on.

Worth knowing where to look: we run no servers and hold no copy of your data, so there is nothing of ours behind the app to attack. What matters is the app itself, what it writes to your device, what it puts into your own iCloud account, and how a shared dive behaves on the receiving side. Findings there are exactly what we want to hear about.

We are too small to offer a bounty and will not pretend otherwise. What we can offer is a straight answer, a fix, and credit in the release notes if you want it.

For how we handle personal information more generally — what is collected, where it goes, and what we can and cannot see — read the privacy policy.
