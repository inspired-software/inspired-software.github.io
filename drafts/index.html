Inspired Software, LLC

We build the hard parts ourselves.

A small studio making native software for Apple platforms — and writing the layers underneath it too. What keeps your dives in step across your devices, what puts the colour back into a photo taken at 30 metres, what works out your decompression: we built those, not just the app sitting on top of them.

Our first app is Deeplife, a dive log for divers who take their photographs as seriously as their logbook.

Meet Deeplife How it’s built

The first app

Deeplife

A dive log that keeps every photo you have ever taken underwater and puts a real Lightroom-grade editor behind them — because the shot that comes back from 30 metres always needs the colour put back.

{{platform-strip}}
  • A logbook that syncs itself. Everything is written to your device first, then mirrored into your own iCloud. No signal needed, which is just as well on most dive trips.
  • A darkroom for water. Tone curve, eight-band colour mixer, masked local adjustments, a heal tool for backscatter, and a dehaze built for the haze water actually produces.
  • A field guide that knows where you are. 120 species and 770+ named dive sites, so when you go to tag the shark you photographed at Monad Shoal, the app already knows what lives there.
  • Planned to follow: a dive computer on your wrist. Not in the first release, and there is no date for it — but it is built and tested. Bühlmann ZH-L16C with gradient factors, live depth from the Apple Watch Ultra’s submersion sensor, and the finished dive logged with one tap.

Warning. The dive computer is experimental and has not been validated for real diving. Never dive without a commercially certified dive computer.

How we work

Three commitments, and we hold to them

They rule out a lot of easy shortcuts. That is rather the point.

Native, not nearly-native

Each version is written for the device it runs on, in the way that device already works. Apple TV gets shelves, the iPad puts the dive list, the dive and the editor side by side, and Apple Vision Pro gets a wreck you can walk around.

Works with no signal

The copy on your device is the real one. It mirrors into your own iCloud account — we run no servers, hold no copy, and there is nothing to log into. On a liveaboard with no bars of signal, everything still works.

Worked out on your device

When the app suggests a species, that will be worked out on your own device, not on ours — no photo of yours is sent to us to be looked at. And the part that would do the suggesting has not been trained yet: until it is, it says so rather than guessing at fish it was never taught.

Underneath

Most of Deeplife is the part we had to build first

A dive log needs storage that carries on out of range and catches up when the boat gets back in. A dive computer needs a decompression model. A dehaze has to cope with water, which is not what dehaze was written for. None of those came off a shelf.

So there are three pieces under the app, and all three are ours. Storage and sync: your dives, photos and edits held on the device, mirrored into your own iCloud, and the same on every screen you own — small versions of your photos travel with the log so the grids fill straight away, and the full-size ones download when you open them. Decompression: Bühlmann ZH-L16C with gradient factors, plus the groundwork for reading dives off a dive computer, which is planned rather than finished. Imaging: the colour cast, the haze and the backscatter that come of photographing in water — three things an ordinary photo editor has no reason to get right.
